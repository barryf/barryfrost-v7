Hello, I'm Barry, and you're reading my personal website. You can find out about me on this page, or read about how this website was [built](/colophon).

<Divider />

I've been [working](/work) with product and technology teams for over 25 years. I started my career as a software engineer, and I continue to tinker with side projects in my spare time. For a while though, I've been leading software engineering teams and functions in companies large and small.

I'm married with two sons. My wife teaches at the same primary school where the boys go, just a five-minute walk from home, while I work from home.

We currently live in a village in Hertfordshire, UK, but I grew up in [Chelmsford](https://www.chelmsfordforyou.co.uk/). After university at [Warwick](https://warwick.ac.uk/) in the West Midlands, I lived in Auckland, Wellington, Battersea, Wimbledon, Winchmore Hill, Twickenham, Windsor, and Virginia Water.

## Interests

Let’s start with the obvious. My first computer was an [Acorn Electron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acorn_Electron) which my parents bought when I was six years old. So began a lifelong obsession. I wrote BASIC to loop printing "BARRY IS THE BEST" and learned programming by typing in code from magazines and saving them to cassettes. I moved up to the Archimedes: a [BBC A3000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acorn_Archimedes#A3000) and then an [A5000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acorn_Archimedes#A5000_and_A4_laptop) on which I built simple WIMP applications. Following Acorn's sad demise, I had a brief affair with a Dell PC before switching to an iBook, iMacs and MacBooks. I've been all-Apple since 2004.

I love the web. I've made a living from building websites, and I've been learning in my spare time ever since I first used dial-up in the 1980s. While still at school, I stumbled upon [ColdFusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adobe_ColdFusion) as a way of building dynamic websites using a database. This knowledge helped gain me my first jobs as a graduate, and although I've long since moved on to newer technologies, I still have a soft spot for CF.

I've been following the [IndieWeb](https://indieweb.org/) movement for over a decade, implementing open standards like [Micropub](https://micropub.spec.indieweb.org/) and [Webmention](https://www.w3.org/TR/webmention/) into my personal websites, attending an [IndieWebCamp](https://indieweb.org/2015/Cambridge) and co-organising a meet-up. Lately I've been diving into the [AT Protocol](https://atproto.com/), on which I've [built](/colophon) this iteration of my personal website. My philosophy is that you should own your data, and strive to build fast, unfussy, accessible websites.

I'm equally excited and worried for the future with AI. Recent models are far better programmers than me, although I can still spot issues from my experience. I hope for a future where my teams are augmented with AI rather than replaced.

I find I'm less interested in computer games these days. I don't have the time to invest. My favourites are from the [SNES](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Hardware/Nintendo-History/Super-Nintendo/Super-Nintendo-627040.html?srsltid=AfmBOoq_W_ivoDklcwhqq1JMpBpD-YGOAPjjcSDpJgF4vzodH-LBQS7H) era, like [The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Games/Super-Nintendo/The-Legend-of-Zelda-A-Link-to-the-Past-841179.html) and [Super Mario World](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Games/Super-Nintendo/Super-Mario-World-752133.html). I spent hours and hours on [Sensible Soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensible_Soccer), and then, [Football Manager](https://www.sports-interactive.com/games/football-manager).

## Sport

I'm an [Arsenal](https://www.arsenal.com/) fan. I get down to The Emirates as often as the Red ticket ballot allows, otherwise I'm a tormented armchair supporter. My very first season going to Highbury as a Junior Gunner was 1988-89, the year Arsenal famously [won the League at Anfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_0%E2%80%932_Arsenal_(1989)) in the dying seconds of the last match. I was hooked for life. At the time of writing, Arsenal are finally [Premier League champions](https://www.arsenal.com/news/arsenal-crowned-202526-premier-league-champions-aOhcw4T06mFw) again, so I'm basking in the warm glow of success.

I love a sunny day at the Oval or Lord's watching cricket, even if England stumble once again. I've been to [dramatic Ashes wins](https://www.cricinfo.com/series/australia-tour-of-england-and-scotland-2005-139019/england-vs-australia-5th-test-218383/live-cricket-score) and [farcical ODI defeats](http://weeknotes/week-47). Live cricket is the best. I've also seen a fair few Six Nations tests at Twickenham and rugby matches around the world. And I've been to Ally Pally to watch the Masters snooker for several years now.

I love watching my sons play football and cricket. My weak sporting abilities are thankfully not genetic, and their trophy-count far exceeds mine. I still pull on my football boots for a kick-around in the park with them, and use my footballing knowledge with [FPL](https://fantasy.premierleague.com/) instead.

## Music

I'm picky about music. Like many people, my [listening habits](https://www.last.fm/user/barryf) are focused on bands I loved in my teenage years and early twenties, like Blur, Suede, Radiohead, Teenage Fanclub, Gene, The Bluetones, Super Furry Animals, and all the way back to Pearl Jam.

I don't go to as many [gigs](https://www.last.fm/user/barryf/events) as I used to, but I make the effort to travel into London, often to see those nineties bands that are still touring years later, or have reformed to cater to middle-aged [6music Dads](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2023/aug/08/bbc-6-music-radio-station) like me. It's hard to beat a festival afternoon with a beer, watching a band you like as the sun sets.

## Travel

I've travelled a bit over the years. In alphabetical order, I've visited: Australia, Belgium, Fiji, France, Germany, Greece, Ireland, Italy, Maldives, Netherlands, New Zealand, Portugal, Serbia, Singapore, South Africa, Spain, Thailand, UAE, and United States.

Some of those countries were part of a year travelling around the world after I graduated, during which I posted to my [Travelblog](/travelblog). I lived and worked in New Zealand for almost a year, and still have very fond memories of my time in Auckland and Wellington.
